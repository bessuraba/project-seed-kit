# Fake door / smoke test — demand testing without building

**Reference material — rarely changes.** Another alternative to live interviews
(`customer-interviews.md`, `netnography.md`) — instead of asking people what they'd do, watch what
they actually do when offered a real (but not-yet-built) choice.

**Source**: Lean Startup canon (classic examples: Dropbox's explainer-video test, Zappos' fake
inventory test).

## What it is

Put up something that looks like a real offer — a landing page, a "Buy"/"Notify me" button, a
waitlist signup describing the actual product — before the product (or full feature) exists.
Measure who takes the next real step. The core discipline, same spirit as Mom Test's "good data vs.
bad data" distinction: **"test the commitment, not the compliment."** Page views, likes, and vague
enthusiasm aren't signal; an email left, a signup completed, a real click on "buy this" is.

## Where it fits vs. interviews and netnography

- Interviews and netnography find out *why* and *what people already do*.
- A fake door test finds out *whether people act* on a specific, concrete offer — closer to a
  real purchase decision than anything people say in an interview, because it costs them something
  (time, an email address) to respond.
- Weakest at explaining *why* — a fake door test that gets no signups tells you demand is weak, not
  *why* it's weak. Pair with interviews/netnography to interpret a result, not to replace them.
