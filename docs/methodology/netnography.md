# Netnography — studying/participating in existing online communities

**Reference material — rarely changes.** Alternative to live customer interviews
(`customer-interviews.md`) when 1:1 interviews aren't feasible: study or lightly participate in
communities where the target users already gather, instead of scheduling calls.

**Source**: Netnography (Robert Kozinets) — an established academic qualitative-research method,
adapting ethnography to online communities. Confirmed via [Wikipedia's netnography
article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netnography).

## What it actually is

Two related techniques, both valid:

1. **Passive mining** — read existing threads/posts as unprompted, naturalistic data. People reveal
   real problems and reactions voluntarily, without a researcher's question shaping the answer.
2. **Active participation** — post a real question into the community and read the responses. Less
   scripted than a live interview, but still real people responding to a real question, not a
   simulated one.

## Why it's a real alternative to interviews, not a lesser substitute

Kozinets' comparison: netnography is **more naturalistic** than interviews/surveys precisely because
there's no researcher present shaping the answer in real time — people say what they'd say anyway.
Tradeoff: less control, no follow-up questions on demand, and it's fundamentally observational or
async — you can't dig into a specific vague answer the way a live interview allows.

For active participation (posting a real question), apply the same Mom Test discipline from
`customer-interviews.md`: ask about people's past decisions/behavior ("how did you decide what to
build last time"), not hypothetical reactions to a specific product idea ("would you use X").
