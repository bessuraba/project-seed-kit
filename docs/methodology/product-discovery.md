# Product discovery & GTM frameworks (stable reference)

**Reference material — rarely changes.** How people proven-successfully approach product
discovery and go-to-market, researched broadly then kept here for reuse across any future
research/planning session.

### Lean Startup (Eric Ries) / Customer Development (Steve Blank)
Validate before building at scale. Build → Measure → Learn loop. MVP tests a core assumption
cheaply before committing real resources. Blank's version: get out of the building — customer
discovery, then validation, then creation, then scale — as sequential gates, not a single kickoff
meeting.

### Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen / Bob Moesta)
Don't ask what feature someone wants — find the "job" they're "hiring" the product to do
(functional, emotional, and social dimensions).

### Continuous Discovery Habits / Opportunity Solution Tree (Teresa Torres)
Current industry-standard discovery practice, not a one-off phase. Structure: one outcome →
customer opportunities (needs/pain points, from real conversations) → candidate solutions →
small assumption tests. Practiced weekly, ongoing — not "do discovery once, then build."

### Positioning — 10-step process (April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*)
1. Understand who already loves the product and why.
2. Get a team aligned on positioning, not one person's gut call.
3. Agree on vocabulary; drop inherited assumptions.
4. List *true* competitive alternatives (including "do nothing" / do it manually).
5. Isolate unique attributes vs. those alternatives.
6. Map attributes to value themes customers actually care about.
7. Determine who cares the most about those themes (best-fit segment).
8. Choose the market frame of reference that puts your strengths at the center.
9–10. (Trim to a positioning statement; test it against real prospects.)

### Beachhead + bowling pin (Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm*)
Don't attack several segments at once. Pick one narrow, winnable segment and dominate it
completely first — only then expand into adjacent segments, using the first as leverage (knock
over one pin, it takes the next). Spreading across segments early is a common, named failure mode,
not a neutral choice.

### PMF quantification — Sean Ellis 40% test
Survey real users: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this?" (very disappointed /
somewhat / not / N/A). ≥40% "very disappointed" correlates strongly with products that go on to
grow; below it, most don't. Needs an actual user base to run.

### GTM structure (general B2B/SaaS)
Five components: ICP, positioning, pricing, channel, team. Best practice grounds ICP in ~15-20 real
customer interviews plus a TAM sanity check, not assumption.
