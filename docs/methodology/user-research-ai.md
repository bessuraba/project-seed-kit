# User research in the AI era (checked 2026-09-04, web-researched)

Classic design thinking places user research in the Empathize/Define stage as a discrete phase.
**No single named, industry-standard methodology has replaced or formally extended that stage for
AI** — nothing with the canonical status JTBD or Lean Startup have. What exists instead is
converging practitioner guidance from credible independent sources (chiefly [Nielsen Norman
Group](https://www.nngroup.com/) and Teresa Torres/Product Talk, the same authority already cited
in `product-discovery.md` for Continuous Discovery). Treat the points below as current best
practice, not a fixed framework — this is an active, fast-moving area and worth re-checking
periodically rather than treating as settled.

**Nearest thing to a named model** — NN/g's [AI roles in
UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-roles-ux/) taxonomy: AI as *Content Editor, Research
Assistant, Design Assistant, Ideation Partner*. "Research Assistant" is the operative role for user
research — desk research, protocol drafting, recruitment comms, transcription/summarization,
reporting.

**Mature, low-risk uses of AI in research today** (NN/g-endorsed): transcription, translation, and
initial-pass summarization/coding of interview data; drafting study plans, interview guides,
screener/recruitment copy; searching a past-research repository. Treat these as safe to lean on.

**Still risky, explicitly warned against**: AI moderating usability tests solo ("AI research tools
are not (yet) capable of actually knowing what users are doing" — NN/g); running full thematic
analysis/synthesis without human review (risk of hallucinated quotes, missed context, "manufactured
insights"). Vendor claims of autonomous AI moderation (e.g. Maze) go further than independent
sources currently validate — treat as marketing, not settled practice.

**"Synthetic users" (AI-simulated respondents) — the live controversy.** NN/g's definition: *"an
AI-generated profile mimicking user groups and providing artificial research findings without
studying real people."* Consensus is hybrid-use, not replacement: sanctioned for early desk
research, hypothesis generation, and interview-guide drafting in an unfamiliar domain; explicitly
**not** sanctioned for validating concepts or replacing real-user research, especially for niche
populations the model has thin training data on. Known failure mode: sycophancy bias — synthetic
respondents behave more agreeably and complete tasks more readily than real users do. One survey
(User Interviews' "State of Synthetic Users," via secondary coverage — directional, not verified
primary data) found 97% of researchers already use AI in their work, but only ~8% trust
AI-generated participants as a stand-in for real ones. Teresa Torres has publicly walked back an
earlier hard "no": she now uses AI as a "fourth perspective" alongside human synthesis, but still
holds that empathy and validation require real human interaction.

**On extending Double Diamond/Design Thinking itself**: no institutionally adopted AI-specific
revision exists (Design Council's own retrospective on the Double Diamond doesn't issue one).
Scattered proposals exist (e.g. an "AIDE" framework, a consultancy's "Stingray Model") but neither
has reached consensus adoption — don't cite either as established. The credible framing is that the
Empathize/Define stage is being *augmented at the tooling/practice level*, not replaced by a new
named methodology.

**Applying this**: keep any user-research synthesis grounded in real sources (forums, communities,
founder statements, actual interviews) — that's the credible-source pattern above, not the risky
one. If AI-assisted synthesis is used on raw interview/forum data, treat it as a first-pass draft
for human review (the NN/g-endorsed use), not a substitute for reading the raw material; don't
generate synthetic-user personas as a stand-in for real evidence.
